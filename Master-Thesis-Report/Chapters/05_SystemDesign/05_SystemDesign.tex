This chapter presents the architecture of the Reading the Reader platform, which is the central contribution of this thesis.
At its core the platform separates adaptive reading into four independently replaceable concerns (sensing, eye-movement analysis, decision, and intervention), joined by a bounded real-time loop and backed by a single authoritative session record, and it runs across two coupled surfaces, a reading surface for the participant and a researcher console that mirrors the participant's reading and steers the session.
The chapter develops this architecture from the top down, beginning with the forces that shaped it and descending through progressively more detailed views to the seams through which it may be extended.
Consistent with the design-science orientation set out in \cref{sec:research-approach}, the emphasis throughout is on justifying these structures against the requirements of \cref{ch:requirements} rather than merely describing them, so that the chapter answers the research questions of \cref{sec:final-problem-statement} as it proceeds.
\Cref{sec:design-drivers} isolates the architecturally significant requirements as a set of design drivers; \cref{sec:design-style} fixes the overall shape of the system and the architectural style it follows; \cref{sec:design-modules} develops the four-layer modular decomposition that carries the central claim; \cref{sec:design-loop} sets the four concerns in motion as the real-time adaptive loop and places each adaptation under the researcher's control; \cref{sec:design-data} describes the authoritative session record and the reproducibility it affords; \cref{sec:design-extensibility} shows how the architecture is opened for extension through its seams and how the researcher console controls a running session; and \cref{sec:design-decisions} consolidates the design decisions and their rationale.
