\section{Extensibility and Researcher Control}
\label{sec:design-extensibility}
% Authoring brief for this section lives in section.md (§5.6).
\Cref{sec:design-modules} declared the seams between the four concerns, and \cref{sec:design-loop,sec:design-data} showed the platform exercising its own built-in implementations behind them.
This section shows those seams used by others.
Two paths extend the platform, and the architecture is arranged so that both are additive: neither requires a change to the reading runtime or to the existing modules.

The two paths differ in where the new behaviour runs.
A new implementation may be added in-process, behind an existing port, so that it executes inside the platform alongside the built-in modules.
Alternatively, an entire concern may be delegated out-of-process to an external provider that runs as a separate program and communicates with the platform across a uniform seam.
The first path is how the platform's own catalogue of interventions and strategies grows; the second is how the decision intelligence that this thesis holds out of scope (C3) can later be supplied by another team.

Adding a new intervention is the smallest unit of extension and illustrates the in-process path in full.
A contributor implements the intervention contract of \cref{sec:design-modules}, providing the descriptor that identifies the module, the validation that checks a request against the current context, and the execution that commits the change, and then registers the implementation with the container.
From that point the registry the runtime consults discovers the module by its identity, and the module becomes available to the decision layer and selectable by the operator.
No part of the reading runtime, the adaptive loop, or any other module is edited to accommodate it, so the intervention is present solely because it was registered, which is precisely the additive extension that RQ1 requires.

The out-of-process path is the architectural answer to constraint C3, which places end-to-end decision intelligence outside the scope of this thesis.
Rather than embed such intelligence, the platform exposes a uniform module-provider seam through which an external program can supply a concern, so that the thesis's responsibility is to make that seam well-defined rather than to implement what crosses it.
A concern that may be externalised contributes two small pieces: a module definition that names the seam and the messages that cross it, and an inbound handler that interprets what a provider sends back.
An external provider, running as a separate process, connects over a provider transport and registers itself as the active provider for that module's identity.

With a provider attached, the seam routes a concern out and back, as \cref{fig:design-seam} traces.
When the core reaches the seam it asks the gateway whether a provider is active for the module in question; if one is, it publishes the current context to that provider and later ingests, through the inbound handler, the proposal the provider returns.
If no provider is active, the gateway reports as much and the same concern is served by its in-process built-in implementation, so the two paths meet at a single outcome.
Because the seam is keyed by module identity and falls back to the built-in whenever no provider is attached, an external provider is a genuine plug-in: the loop of \cref{sec:design-loop} runs unchanged whether one is present or absent, which is the graceful behaviour required of live operation (D3).

The decision concern is the seam most likely to be supplied externally, so it is worth making concrete what crosses it.
What the platform sends out is context: the authoritative session snapshot together with the stream of analysed signals it derives, namely the reading focus, the attention summary, and the oculomotor events of \cref{sec:design-loop}.
What it expects back is a decision, expressed as a proposal that pairs an observed signal and a short, human-readable rationale with a concrete intervention, that is, the identity of an intervention module and the parameters to apply.
The seam therefore commits the platform to a vocabulary, signals in and a proposal out, but not to any particular way of arriving at the proposal.

That deliberate silence about how a decision is reached is what makes the concern pluggable.
The built-in provider reaches its proposal by a simple threshold heuristic over the analysed signals, which is enough to exercise the loop from end to end, whereas an external provider may reach the same kind of proposal by any means it chooses, for example a model trained to classify reading patterns from gaze.
Supplying such a model is the end-to-end decision intelligence that constraint C3 places out of scope; the architectural contribution is the seam that makes it integrable, the context it receives and the proposal it must return, rather than the algorithm behind it.

The seam of \cref{fig:design-seam} carries a concern out and back within a single session, but together with the session record of \cref{sec:design-data} the same seam opens a second, longer loop that spans sessions.
An external decision provider improves only insofar as it can learn from prior data, and the authoritative record is precisely such data.
Each completed session seals one schema-versioned record (\cref{fig:design-record}), and exported in a portable form these records accumulate across a study into a corpus of past sessions.
A provider may therefore be fine-tuned offline on that corpus and then reattached through the very same decision seam, so that an improved provider drives interventions in later sessions without any change to the reading runtime or to the existing modules.

We are deliberate about which parts of this loop the thesis realises and which it only affords.
The reproducible record and its export, and the seam through which a provider attaches, are built and exercised in the platform, whereas the learning that would consume the corpus is external and, under constraint C3, out of scope.
What the architecture contributes is therefore not the intelligence itself but the two structures that make such intelligence improvable in place: a record complete enough to learn from, which is the reproducibility argued in \cref{sec:design-data}, and a seam uniform enough that a retrained provider re-enters the loop without disturbance, which is the additive extension required by RQ1.
In this sense the platform is not only adaptive within a session but improvable across sessions, and that longer-horizon capability is the deeper reason the external-provider seam is worth its cost.

Extensibility opens the platform to future module providers; a second structure, the researcher console, opens it to the researcher who runs the experiment, and it too is an architectural concern rather than a matter of screen layout.
The architecture separates the researcher's console from the participant's reading surface, the second-screen arrangement introduced in \cref{sec:design-style}, so that the participant sees only the text while the researcher retains the controls and a mirror of the participant's view.
It gates a session behind explicit readiness, requiring device selection, calibration, and validation to succeed before a run can begin, so that a session cannot be started in a mis-configured state.
And it derives the console from the single authoritative session snapshot of \cref{sec:design-data}, so that the operator commands and observes the same state through a single console rather than a collection of disconnected controls.
Through this console the researcher exercises the manual, advisory, and autonomous control developed in \cref{sec:design-loop} and reviews the proposals and gaze overlays mirrored from the participant's session.

The console gates and drives a session through a defined lifecycle, shown in \cref{fig:design-session-lifecycle}, which gathers the structures of this chapter into the order a researcher experiences them.
A session begins in configuration, where four setup prerequisites are assembled, namely a saved participant, a selected and licensed eye tracker, a passed calibration, and chosen reading material, and the readiness gate forbids a start until all of them hold, so that a session cannot run in a mis-configured state (D6).
For a simulated sensing source the eye-tracker and calibration prerequisites fall away (D5), which is what allows the lifecycle to be exercised without hardware.
Once ready, the researcher starts the session and the real-time adaptive loop of \cref{sec:design-loop} runs, writing the session record incrementally as it proceeds.
Should the session be disturbed, by a crash or a disconnect, the checkpointed record leaves it recoverable up to the last checkpoint, so that the loop resumes rather than the session being lost (D3).
When the researcher finishes, the record is sealed and exported (\cref{sec:design-data}), after which the platform is reset for the next participant.
This lifecycle is the researcher's view of the architecture in operation, and it is the structure through which the operator-control driver D6 is ultimately discharged.

With the extension seams and the researcher console, the structures that make up the architecture are now all in view.
What remains is to draw together the decisions that produced them, each with the alternatives it was chosen over, and \cref{sec:design-decisions} gathers these into a single register.
