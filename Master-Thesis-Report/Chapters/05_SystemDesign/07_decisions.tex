Read together, the register makes the chapter's recurring tension explicit.
Several decisions (DR3, DR7, and the fallback within DR4) trade a measure of architectural purity for responsiveness or robustness, while others (DR1, DR2, DR8) hold the separation of concerns firm; none of these is an accident of implementation, and each is a deliberate position taken in view of the drivers.
That the trade-offs are visible and motivated, rather than hidden, is what we intend by presenting the architecture as argued rather than narrated.

\section{Technology Selection}
\label{sec:technology-selection}
The decisions above concern structure and are deliberately independent of any particular language or framework; this section records the technologies chosen to realise that structure and the reasoning behind them.
Two principles guided the selection.
First, the technologies serve the drivers rather than dictate the architecture: the ports-and-adapters structure of \cref{sec:design-modules} could be realised on several stacks, so the choice is a matter of fit and cost rather than capability.
Second, under the time constraints of a thesis, the team's existing proficiency was treated as a first-class criterion, since building on familiar tools directed effort towards the architecture and the adaptive runtime rather than towards learning new frameworks.

Two factors narrowed the field beyond familiarity.
First, the Tobii Research SDK is distributed with official language bindings only for .NET and Python \cite{tobii2025fusion}, which limited the backend to one of these two; a JavaScript or other stack would have required an interop bridge to a supported binding.
Between the two, .NET was chosen over Python for the team's greater proficiency with C\# and for its mature asynchronous runtime (D2), with the Windows-only distribution of the SDK already fixed as constraint C1.
Second, because the frontend and backend contracts are maintained by hand rather than generated, TypeScript's static typing was valued for catching contract drift at compile time, which supports reproducibility (D4).
Beyond these, the alternatives in \cref{tab:technology-selection} were judged broadly capable; none offered an advantage that outweighed the team's proficiency with the chosen stack.
We note for honesty that the chosen frontend framework's App Router later proved convenient for separating the researcher and participant surfaces, but this was a consequence of the choice rather than a reason for it.
