\begin{abstract}
Real-time gaze-driven text adaptation has been demonstrated repeatedly, yet studying which adaptations actually help readers remains expensive, because existing prototypes couple sensing, decision logic, and the reading interface into single-purpose codebases.
We present a researcher-operated platform in which sensing, eye-movement analysis, decision strategy, and intervention execution are independently replaceable modules behind stable contracts: a participant reads reflowable text that adapts to their gaze while a researcher mirrors the session live and applies, approves, or delegates typographic interventions, with every sample, decision, and intervention captured in a replayable session record.
Across eight sessions on research-grade eye-tracking hardware, the platform sustained the tracker's rated 90\,Hz, kept every measured round trip within its 100\,ms budget, and had all sessions analysed live by an independent team's reading-difficulty pipeline connected through the provider contract without modifying the core.
Because the platform measures the positional cost of every intervention it applies, it exposed that our original context-preserving restore moved readers roughly three times further than the reflow it compensated; a revised restore, verified over a 66-trial deterministic sweep, brought median displacement below the no-preservation baseline.
A work-in-progress webcam module that derives facial state from commodity cameras behind the same sensing contract sketches a path toward low-cost deployment, and the platform and all session data are open source.
\end{abstract}
