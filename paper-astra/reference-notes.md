# Initial reference verification

Checked on 6 September 2026 after reviewing the local thesis and implementation. This is an initial relevance and metadata check, not the completed related-work review. No existing repository paper draft was consulted. The proposed uses below are editorial judgments based on the retrieved source material.

## Supervisor-suggested sources

1. **Jensen et al. (2025), “Context Preservation Through Eye Tracking: Adaptive Reading Application Design for an Optimal Reading Experience.”** DOI: [10.1145/3715669.3725897](https://doi.org/10.1145/3715669.3725897). The [DTU record](https://orbit.dtu.dk/en/publications/context-preservation-through-eye-tracking-adaptive-reading-applic/) identifies ETRA 2025, article 78, eight pages, and a 22-participant within-subjects experiment. The [primary PDF](https://backend.orbit.dtu.dk/ws/portalfiles/portal/406853513/3715669.3725897.pdf) was accessed and its design/results excerpts inspected. Use it to understand the lineage and how context preservation and recovery are framed. Its highlight/no-highlight comparison draws on an earlier study; do not describe all of those conditions as a single concurrent randomised comparison. For our paper, the distinction is the configurable research instrument and its evidence, rather than a repetition of its highlight intervention study.

2. **Rummens and Beier, “Gaze-based word highlighting boosts reading performance: an eye tracking study in second graders.”** DOI: [10.1080/00140139.2025.2514600](https://doi.org/10.1080/00140139.2025.2514600). The [publisher page](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/00140139.2025.2514600) reports online publication on 12 June 2025 and final placement in *Ergonomics* 69(7), 2026, pp. 1253–1265. Choose one consistent bibliographic version when building BibTeX. Its abstract reports 70 Danish second graders, dynamic word colouring, performance and comprehension measures, and a preference for static text despite efficiency gains. Use the distinction between performance and preference as a reporting model. This review accessed metadata and abstract material, not the full methods/results behind the publisher access boundary; full close reading remains part of drafting preparation. Its findings cannot establish benefit for this platform or this cohort.

3. **Kaltenberger, Chen, Thaqi and Kasneci (2026), “Sure About That Line? Approaching Confidence-Based, Real-Time Line Assignment in Reading Gaze Data.”** [arXiv:2605.00033](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.00033). The primary arXiv record verifies the title and authors. Use it for the uncertainty in online line assignment. Our current mapper has a fixed line preference and does not implement this method, probabilistic confidence, or a validated improvement over it. Abstract/metadata checked; full algorithm comparison remains to be done. Do not equate an algorithm's per-fixation runtime with the platform's client RTT or latest-gaze freshness metric.

4. **Medan and Pelman (2024), “New line: A new gaze-contingent digital reading interface.”** The [conference-hosted paper](https://meitalconf.iucc.ac.il/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/MeitalConference_2024_paper_14.pdf) is accessible. Its opening describes a gaze-scrolled continuous line with focused words emphasised, and a 13-participant pilot without significant speed or comprehension differences. Use it to compare the interaction offered to readers. Its existence also makes a broad claim that gaze-contingent reading platforms do not exist untenable. Publication metadata beyond the conference-hosted 2024 paper should be completed before creating the bibliography entry.

5. **Ilyas et al. (2025), “Reading the Readers Mind through Eye Tracking: Can AI Generated Texts Match Human Authors?”** DOI: [10.1145/3715669.3726846](https://doi.org/10.1145/3715669.3726846). The [DTU record](https://orbit.dtu.dk/en/publications/reading-the-readers-mind-through-eye-tracking-can-ai-generated-te/) verifies ETRA 2025, article 113; the [primary PDF](https://backend.orbit.dtu.dk/ws/portalfiles/portal/406853299/3715669.3726846.pdf) identifies a 13-participant comparison and I2MC fixation analysis. Use it as an example of reporting reading materials and gaze-derived measures to this venue. AI-authored stimuli are a different contribution from an AI decision provider. Metadata and opening material checked; no claim is made that our system reproduces its method or results.

## A necessary addition to the comparison

The thesis's capability matrix needs a new source-based review before reuse. Official [PsychoPy ioHub documentation](https://devdocs.psychopy.org/api/iohub/device/eyetracker.html) describes a common interface across several tracker manufacturers, including a simulated source; [MouseGaze documentation](https://psychopy.org/api/iohub/device/eyetracker_interface/MouseGaze_Implementation_Notes.html) explicitly documents mouse simulation. Hardware abstraction and simulated gaze should therefore not be presented as unique inventions of this thesis.

Compare the specific out-of-the-box reading workflow, intervention contracts, researcher proposal handling, and records. Distinguish built-in capabilities, capabilities requiring custom programming, and capabilities not documented. Complete the same primary-source check for the other general experiment systems before assigning capability ratings. This check did not examine venue deadlines or submission rules.
