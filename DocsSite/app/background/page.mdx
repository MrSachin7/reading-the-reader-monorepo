## Eye tracking in reading, in three sentences

Gaze during reading decomposes into discrete oculomotor events: **fixations** (the eye dwells on a word and extracts information), **saccades** (rapid jumps between fixations), and **regressions** (backward jumps, commonly associated with processing difficulty). Decades of reading research link these events to ongoing comprehension, which is what makes them a usable live signal for adaptation. Extracting them from a raw gaze stream requires an explicit identification algorithm; this platform uses a dwell-based, area-of-interest method (I-AOI in the Salvucci and Goldberg taxonomy), described in the [Sensing Layer](/system-design/sensing-layer/).

## Prior work in the Reading the Reader project

Earlier student projects in the programme proved that gaze-driven adaptive reading works: a flexible reading and data-collection application, an adaptive e-reader comparing four intervention designs, and a reactive frontend for ML-driven adaptation. Each was a capable prototype, but each was extended by copying the previous codebase rather than by building against a stable contract, and none offered a researcher console, controlled experiment operation, or a reproducible session record. That coupling gap is the problem this platform addresses.

## Existing tools

Research-grade eye trackers and experiment suites (Tobii Pro Lab, PsychoPy, Psychtoolbox) excel at experiment design, stimulus delivery, and gaze capture, but none closes the loop from live gaze to a real-time typographic change, and none exposes a pluggable decision boundary. Accessibility tools (Immersive Reader, e-reader settings) adapt presentation, but only as manual, static customisation. The combination this platform targets, real-time gaze sensing plus automatic typographic adaptation plus researcher-facing experiment control in one modular system, was unoccupied.
